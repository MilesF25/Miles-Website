What I've Been Up To

Not having a job gave me a strong sense of imposter syndrome. I struggled with LeetCode-style questions, and that affected my confidence because I felt like I wasn't improving in the areas that are often emphasized for software engineering interviews. Instead of constantly focusing on what I couldn't do, I decided to focus on what I knew I could do: build things. I started by revamping my personal website. Having something tangible that I could work on, improve, and be proud of helped me regain some confidence. That small project eventually gave me the motivation to revisit other projects, including my thesis. From there, I started exploring new areas and challenging myself in different ways. While studying for the Security+ certification, I became interested in learning more about cybersecurity and finding ways to apply that knowledge through projects. At the same time, I continued improving older work, like my thesis and personal projects.

BanjoDB

One of the biggest struggles I ran into was figuring out what projects to work on. If you search for "fun coding projects," you usually get the same suggestions over and over again: a weather app, a calculator, a to-do list, and other beginner projects. There's nothing wrong with those projects, they are great for learning the basics but for me, they were difficult to stay motivated on because I wasn't genuinely interested in building them. I tried starting some of those projects, but I couldn't force myself to finish them. Without a real interest or problem I wanted to solve, it was hard to stay committed. That realization pushed me to revisit projects that actually mattered to me. One of those projects was my thesis. I wanted to redo it because I wasn't happy with how it turned out the first time. A series of unfortunate events prevented me from creating something I was truly proud of, and I wanted the chance to improve it. The database I built for it gave me an opportunity to explore areas I was curious about, such as database security, encryption, and becoming more familiar with Rust. This project allowed me to combine Python and Rust into a single application, which was something new and challenging for me. Overall, I really enjoyed working on it. It was a difficult project, and i can't say I'm that proud of it but it pushed me outside of my comfort zone and forced me to learn new concepts. More importantly, it reminded me why I enjoy programming in the first, I like seeing my ideas come to life. Project link: Project link:BanjoDB

The Server

While studying for my CompTIA Security+ exam, I became more interested in the lower-level side of cybersecurity. I found myself wondering how the tools we rely on actually work behind the scenes. How does a firewall know what to block? How does a server handle multiple connections? This project was my attempt to answer those questions by building my own LAN server from scratch. This was one of the hardest projects I have worked on. I had no previous experience with network programming, and learning concepts like asynchronous networking, multithreading, `Arc`, and `Mutex` in Rust at the same time was challenging. There were many moments where I had to stop coding and focus on understanding what was actually happening. The project taught me a lot about how servers work, but more importantly, it showed me that I enjoy tackling difficult problems. Seeing the final result come together was incredibly rewarding, and I am proud of what I was able to build. Project link:Server

What's Next

I'm not exactly sure what my next steps are, but I do know that I have some time to figure them out. I chose the title "Growth and Ongoing Mastery of Now" because it represents where I am at this point in my life. I don't have everything figured out, and I can't control every opportunity that comes my way, but I can control the effort I put into improving myself.

For now, my goal is simple: keep learning, keep building, and keep getting better at what I do. Whether that means improving my programming skills, learning more about cybersecurity, or working on projects that that are fun or challenge me, I want to continue pushing myself forward.

Looking back, I started this journey feeling discouraged and unsure of my abilities. But working on these projects reminded me that progress doesn't always come from getting the perfect opportunity. Sometimes it comes from taking the time to learn, experiment, and create something you're proud of.

I don't know exactly where this path will lead, but I know I want to continue growing. For now, that's enough.
